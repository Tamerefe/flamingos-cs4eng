# S3.4 — Lab: harden FactoryFlow

**Day 3 · 14:00–15:30 · You work in pairs. There are no instructions this time.**

## The brief

> You have a working, tested, containerised application. You have 90 minutes and
> no instructions.
>
> Extend it in whatever direction interests you most. Add a feature. Break
> something on purpose and fix it. Explore a tool you did not have time for. Or
> clean up the rough edges you have been walking past since Tuesday.
>
> At 16:00 you present. The only thing you have to be able to say is **why you
> chose what you chose.**

**One constraint: whatever you do, it must still run at 16:00.**

## 1 · Decide, and write it down (10–20 min)

Before you touch the keyboard, put three things on a card and show it to a
facilitator:

1. **What we are doing.**
2. **What we are explicitly not doing** — at least two things.
3. **How we will know at 15:30 whether it worked.**

Point 2 is the one that saves your afternoon.

Ask each other: *"how long do you think that will take?"* Then double it. Is it
still in scope?

## 2 · Build (20–75 min)

No hint ladder, no skeleton, no checkpoint. That is the session.

The rubric you were given on day 1 is the only guidance.

## 3 · Freeze at 15:15 (15:15–15:25)

**Hands off the code.** Run your tests. Run your container. Commit. Push.

If it is broken now, fix it now — at 15:30 it is what you have.

There is always a point where stopping is the correct engineering decision.
This is what that feels like.

## 4 · Demo prep (85–90 min)

Five minutes. Decide who says what.

## Checkpoint

It still runs, it is committed and pushed, and you can say why you chose this
over the alternatives.
