## Your part — the discussion (15:00–15:20)

Three minutes of silent writing, then eight minutes at your table, then each
table reports one case for and one case against.

> **Think of a real task** from your studies, your internship or your
> working-student job.
>
> Where would you trust an AI system with it, and where would you not?
> **What would have to be true for you to change your mind?**

Write about a task you have actually done. Generic answers produce a generic
discussion.

## The one thing to take away

> **AI is useful in proportion to how cheaply you can verify its output.**

Code is the easy case: it runs, it can be tested, it fails loudly. A demand
forecast has no runtime and no test — you find out in six months. Same model,
completely different risk.

So the question is never *"is the AI good at this?"* It is:

> **"What does it cost me to find out that it was wrong, and when?"**
