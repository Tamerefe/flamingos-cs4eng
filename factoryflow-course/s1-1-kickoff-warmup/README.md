# S1.1 — The first half hour

**Day 1 · 09:00–09:30**

Ten minutes of me talking, ten minutes making teams, ten minutes of a game. Nothing here counts, nothing is assessed, and you do not owe anybody anything at half past nine.

Laptops closed.

## 1 — The three days

What you are building, how the days fit together, and the deal. One slide each, and then we stop.

The part worth remembering: **stuck for fifteen minutes means take the finished version.** No questions, no loss of face. It is on the table for every session of all three days.

## 2 — Your team

You will hear who you are paired with. Sit together.

Then pick a **team name**. Two rules: you have three minutes, and it has to be sayable across a room. Write it big on the folded sheet of paper and stand it on your table — it stays there until Friday evening.

Then, round the room, one at a time:

> Your name, your team name, and **the last thing that broke on you at work**.

One sentence. That is the only thing anybody asks you to say in front of the room today.

## 3 — Shout It

I draw on the whiteboard. You shout what it is.

- First team to say the word out loud gets the point.
- I decide who I heard first, I am always right, and we are never discussing it.
- Twelve drawings, forty seconds each.
- If nobody gets it in forty seconds, I tell you what it was and we move on.

None of the words has anything to do with this workshop. Some of them cannot really be drawn at all, which is the fun part and it is entirely my problem.

## What you get out of it

Nothing measurable. You will have talked to everybody in the room, watched the person running the workshop fail at something in public, and found out that this is fine.

The rest of the three days is easier after that.

## Also on your table

The **AI Rules of Engagement** card. Three lines, and they hold all week:

> **AI is allowed.** Use it like any other tool on the bench.
>
> **One limit.** Nothing from this course — code, task text, hints — gets pasted into it to produce your answer. Ask it what a word means, what an error message is telling you, why a name is bad. That is the tool used well.
>
> **The standard.** If the shift supervisor asks *"what does this line do?"* — you can answer.
