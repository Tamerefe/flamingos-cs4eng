# The showcase rubric

Five minutes of demo, two of questions, per pair. The audience plays sceptical
shift supervisors.

**Hand this out on day 1**, not on day 3 — people build towards what they are
measured on, and here that is the point.

| Dimension | Weight | 1 — not yet | 3 — solid | 5 — excellent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **It runs** | 25 % | Needs the presenter's laptop and luck | Runs from the repo | Runs in a container on a machine that has never seen it |
| **It is trustworthy** | 25 % | No tests | Tests cover the core metrics | Tests cover edge cases and pin down a real bug that was found |
| **It is understandable** | 20 % | Names and structure obscure intent | Clear names, functions do one thing, README works | A stranger could add a feature without asking questions |
| **It answers a real question** | 20 % | Shows data | Shows KPIs a supervisor would use | Reveals an insight the supervisor did not already have |
| **They own it** | 10 % | Cannot explain parts of it | Explains every line asked about | Explains the trade-offs and what they would do differently |

The asymmetry is deliberate: *it runs* and *it is trustworthy* together outweigh
features. That is the thesis of the whole workshop, and the rubric should say so.

**The question the guest supervisor asks every pair:** *"How do you know this
number is right?"*
